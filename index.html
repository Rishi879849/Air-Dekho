Health Risks

Diseases Caused by Bad Air

Prolonged exposure to poor air quality silently damages your body. Know the risks. Protect your family.

🫁

Asthma

Fine particles and ozone trigger severe asthma attacks. Children are especially vulnerable. Even short exposures can cause lasting airway inflammation.

PM2.5 Ozone SO₂
🤧

Chronic Bronchitis

Long-term exposure to pollutants causes persistent cough, mucus production, and breathing difficulty. Smokers and elderly face highest risk in Gwalior's traffic-heavy zones.

NOₓ Dust
💔

Cardiovascular Disease

Air pollution accelerates plaque build-up in arteries, increasing risk of heart attack and stroke. Even moderate AQI levels raise blood pressure significantly over time.

PM2.5 CO
👁️

Eye Irritation & Damage

Airborne pollutants cause redness, burning, and conjunctivitis. Chronic exposure can lead to corneal damage and increased risk of cataracts in later life.

Ozone Smog
🧠

COPD

Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease causes irreversible lung damage. Gwalior's industrial emissions and vehicular pollution are major contributing factors to rising COPD rates.

PM10 NOₓ
🤰

Pregnancy Complications

Exposure to high AQI during pregnancy links to premature birth, low birth weight, and developmental issues in children. Expectant mothers should closely monitor air quality alerts.

PM2.5 Pb

Who Is Most Vulnerable in Gwalior?

Children under 12, elderly above 60, pregnant women, and those with pre-existing respiratory or cardiac conditions face the highest risks. On high AQI days, these groups should stay indoors and limit physical activity.

Prevention Guide

Do's & Don'ts

Simple everyday actions that protect you from air pollution — and habits to break that make it worse for everyone in Gwalior.

Wear an N95 Mask

Use a certified N95 or N99 mask when stepping outdoors on high AQI days. Surgical masks alone do not filter fine PM2.5 particles.

AQI > 100

Keep Windows Closed

On poor AQI days, keep doors and windows shut during peak traffic hours (7–10 AM and 5–8 PM) to reduce indoor pollution.

AQI > 200

Grow Indoor Plants

Plants like Peace Lily, Spider Plant, and Areca Palm naturally filter indoor air toxins like benzene, formaldehyde, and CO₂.

Always

Use Public Transport or Cycle

Reduce personal vehicular emissions by using buses, e-rickshaws, or cycling. Each petrol vehicle off the road drops local AQI measurably.

Always

Use Air Purifiers Indoors

Run a HEPA air purifier in bedrooms and study rooms during high-pollution periods. HEPA filters capture 99.97% of PM2.5 particles.

AQI > 150

Stay Hydrated

Drink at least 8–10 glasses of water daily to help your body flush out toxins absorbed through polluted air. Add tulsi or ginger for respiratory benefit.

Always

Exercise During Low-AQI Hours

In Gwalior, early morning (before 7 AM) typically has lower AQI than evening. Plan outdoor workouts and walks accordingly.

Daily habit

Service Your Vehicle Regularly

A properly tuned engine emits up to 40% fewer pollutants. Replace air filters and get PUC certificates on time. An old, poorly maintained engine is a rolling polluter.

Every 6 months

Check AQI Before Stepping Out

Make it a habit to check the AIR DEKHO dashboard every morning. Share the reading with elderly family members who may not check on their own.

Every morning

Don't Burn Waste or Crop Stubble

Open burning of garbage, dry leaves, or crop residue releases dense PM2.5 and toxic gases that can spike the city AQI by 50–100 points overnight.

Major polluter

Don't Exercise Outdoors on Severe AQI Days

Heavy breathing during exercise forces 10× more air into your lungs. On days with AQI above 200, outdoor physical activity can do more harm than good.

AQI > 200

Don't Smoke Indoors

Indoor smoking raises PM2.5 levels to 10× outdoor pollution. Combined with already poor outdoor AQI in Gwalior, this creates extremely hazardous indoor air for families.

Always avoid

Don't Idle Your Engine

Idling for more than 60 seconds wastes fuel and releases CO and NOₓ directly into street-level air where pedestrians and children breathe. Switch off at red lights.

Always avoid

Don't Use Chemical Sprays Indoors

Aerosol sprays (air fresheners, pesticides, hairsprays) release VOCs that react with indoor air to form secondary pollutants. Use natural alternatives especially in small, unventilated rooms.

Reduce use

Don't Use Coal or Wood for Cooking

Solid fuel combustion indoors releases black carbon and carbon monoxide at dangerous concentrations. Switch to LPG or induction stoves to protect your family.

Always avoid

Don't Let Children Play Near Highways

Traffic corridors like NH-44 and Shivpuri Road have pollution levels 3–5× higher than residential zones. Keep children's play areas away from major roads.

High-risk group

Don't Ignore Symptoms

Persistent cough, itchy eyes, wheezing, or headaches on high-AQI days are warning signs. Don't dismiss them — seek medical advice promptly, especially for children and the elderly.

Act fast

Don't Burst Firecrackers

A single Diwali night of firecrackers can push Gwalior's AQI above 400 — into the hazardous zone. Celebrate with eco-friendly light shows and diyas instead.
